Destination 01

Tokyo
東京

Tokyo is a city of extraordinary contrasts — ancient temples in the shadows of gleaming skyscrapers, the world's busiest pedestrian crossing steps from serene Imperial gardens. With a population of 14 million, it is the world's most populous city, yet operates with remarkable order and grace.

Explore the electric energy of Akihabara's tech district, find peace in Yanaka's preserved old-town lanes, or lose yourself in the seven floors of Shibuya Crossing's neon labyrinth.

Tokyo is home to more Michelin-starred restaurants than any other city on earth — you can eat incredibly well from a vending machine or at a 3-star counter.

🗼 Skytree Tower ⛩️ Meiji Shrine 🛍️ Shibuya 🎮 Akihabara 🌸 Ueno Park
Best Time to Visit

Spring or Autumn

March–May brings cherry blossoms throughout Ueno and Shinjuku parks. October–November turns Tokyo's gingko trees brilliant gold.

Getting Around

The Subway

Tokyo's metro is the most extensive in the world — 13 lines, 285 stations. A SUICA card makes travel effortless. Trains run every 3–5 minutes.

Don't Miss

Tsukiji Market

The world-famous outer fish market opens before dawn. Arrive by 6am for the freshest sushi breakfast of your life, served standing at tiny stalls.

Destination 02

Kyoto
京都

For over a thousand years, Kyoto was the imperial capital of Japan. Today it remains the country's cultural soul — a city where geishas still walk lantern-lit alleys at dusk and Buddhist monks chant before dawn in mountain temples.

Fushimi Inari Taisha's ten thousand vermillion torii gates wind up a forested mountain. The bamboo grove of Arashiyama whispers in the wind. The Golden Pavilion, Kinkaku-ji, shimmers on its reflecting pond.

Kyoto rewards the slow traveller. Stay two or three days at minimum, and walk. Every narrow backstreet hides a garden, a gallery, or a centuries-old tea house.

⛩️ Fushimi Inari 🏯 Kinkaku-ji 🎋 Arashiyama 🍵 Tea Ceremony 🌙 Gion District
Best Time to Visit

Late March

Sakura season transforms Maruyama Park and the Philosopher's Path into tunnels of pale pink petals. Book accommodation 6 months in advance.

Local Experience

A Tea Ceremony

Kyoto is the birthplace of the Japanese tea ceremony. Many machiya townhouses offer 45-minute ceremonies with a matcha bowl and seasonal sweet.

Day Trip

Nara Deer Park

Just 45 minutes from Kyoto by train, over 1,000 wild deer roam freely through Nara's ancient streets and parks, entirely unafraid of visitors.

Destination 03

Mount Fuji
富士山

Rising 3,776 metres above the surrounding plains, Mount Fuji is not merely Japan's highest mountain — it is the nation's most iconic image, a near-perfect volcanic cone that has inspired artists, pilgrims, and poets for centuries.

The climbing season runs July through early September. Around 300,000 people summit each year via four trails, with the Yoshida Trail being the most popular. The summit rewards early risers with the Goraiko — the magnificent sunrise over the clouds below.

Non-climbers can experience Fuji's majesty from the Fuji Five Lakes, where the mountain's reflection shimmers on still morning water.

🏔️ Summit Trek 🌅 Goraiko Sunrise 🏞️ Fuji Five Lakes ♨️ Onsen Spas 🎑 Chureito Pagoda
Climbing Season

July to September

The official climbing season runs from 1 July to 10 September. Outside this window trails close and mountain huts shut — the summit becomes dangerously cold.

Best View

Chureito Pagoda

The iconic image of a five-storey pagoda framed against Fuji and spring cherry blossoms is taken from Arakura Sengen Shrine — 398 stone steps above the town.

After the Climb

Hakone Onsen

Soak in volcanic hot spring baths just 30 minutes from Fuji. Many ryokan inns have outdoor rotenburo baths with direct views of the mountain.
